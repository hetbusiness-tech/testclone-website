## The Challenge

A spray tanning tent isn't an impulse buy. It's a considered purchase — customers want to know how it sets up, what space it needs, whether it fits their skill level, and how it compares to other options before they're ready to commit. This brand had a solid product, but the store wasn't built for that kind of decision-making. Key details were scattered or missing entirely, and comparing options meant more guesswork than it should have.

The result was a familiar pattern: interested visitors who left with more questions than answers, and never came back to finish what they started. Every unanswered question at that stage isn't just a missed sale — it's a customer who was close enough to buy, but not quite convinced enough to commit. For a niche product like this, that gap between "interested" and "ready" was where the business was quietly losing ground.

## The Solution

Technostripe rebuilt the product experience around exactly what buyers needed to know before they'd feel comfortable purchasing.

Specs, setup requirements, and sizing information were pulled out of buried text blocks and built directly into the browsing experience, where customers would actually see them. We restructured product pages so comparing options felt straightforward instead of like extra homework, answering the practical questions upfront instead of leaving them for a support inbox.

## The Outcome

The shift showed up almost immediately in fewer pre-sale questions landing in customer service — a sign that people were finding what they needed on their own.

More importantly, visitors who used to leave uncertain started completing purchases with confidence, because the store had already answered what they came looking for.

## The Takeaway

For considered purchases, hesitation isn't usually about price or interest — it's about missing information. When a store proactively answers the questions a buyer would otherwise have to ask, it doesn't just reduce support load, it moves people from "interested" to "ready" on their own. The best product page isn't the most persuasive one — it's the one that leaves nothing left to ask.
