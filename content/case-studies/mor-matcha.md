# Stopping the Slow Bleed at Checkout

---

## 01 — The Brand

MOR Matcha is a premium matcha brand built around making ceremonial-grade matcha part of an everyday ritual.

The brand already had many of the ingredients needed for a strong D2C business:
- Premium positioning
- Clean product presentation
- Strong photography
- A steady stream of engaged visitors

The problem wasn't getting people interested. **It was getting them across the finish line.**

---

## 02 — The Challenge

**The product was converting attention. The checkout was losing customers.**

More than seven out of every ten customers who added a product to their cart were leaving before completing their purchase.

That meant the hardest part was already happening:
- Customers were discovering the product.
- They were interested enough to add it to their cart.
- They were ready to buy.

But somewhere between *“Add to Cart”* and *“Order Confirmed,”* too many of them were disappearing.

> For a D2C brand, that's more than a UX issue. It's revenue that has already been earned in the customer's mind — but never reaches the business.

![Cart Abandonment — Over 70% of shoppers were dropping off between cart and checkout](/portfolio/mor-4.png)

---

## 03 — The Diagnosis

**The problem wasn't demand. It was friction.**

We looked at the checkout experience to understand why customers who had already decided to buy were still abandoning their carts. Three critical issues stood out:

- **Unexpected Shipping Costs**  
  Shipping information appeared too late in the journey, creating an unwelcome surprise at the final point of purchase.
- **Forced Account Creation**  
  Customers were pushed towards creating an account instead of being able to complete their purchase immediately.
- **Too Many Steps on Mobile**  
  With a large share of traffic coming from mobile devices, every additional interaction created another opportunity for hesitation and abandonment.

The checkout wasn't broken. It was simply asking too much from a customer who had already said yes.

![The Friction Points — Hidden shipping fees, forced account creation, and excessive checkout steps](/portfolio/mor-5.png)

---

## 04 — The Strategy

**Make the path to purchase feel effortless.**

The objective wasn't to redesign the entire brand. It was much more focused: **remove every unnecessary reason for a ready-to-buy customer to stop.**

We rebuilt the checkout experience around four core principles:

- **Transparency** &rarr; Show important costs earlier
- **Simplicity** &rarr; Remove unnecessary form fields and steps
- **Flexibility** &rarr; Let customers purchase without creating an account
- **Mobile-First** &rarr; Make the experience effortless on smaller screens

> **The goal was simple:** `Cart` &rarr; `Checkout` &rarr; `Purchase` with as little friction in between as possible.

---

## 05 — What We Changed

### 01. Earlier Shipping Transparency
Shipping costs were moved earlier in the purchase journey so customers could understand the real cost before reaching the final step.  
*No last-minute surprises.*

### 02. Guest Checkout
We removed the unnecessary account-creation barrier and allowed customers to complete their purchase without committing to an account.  
*Buy first. Account later.*

### 03. A Shorter Checkout Journey
The checkout flow was simplified to reduce unnecessary interactions and make the path from cart to confirmation more direct.  
*Every step had to earn its place.*

### 04. Mobile-First Optimisation
The checkout was refined with mobile behaviour in mind — reducing taps, improving clarity, and making key information easier to understand on a smaller screen.

Because when most of your traffic is on mobile, **checkout friction becomes conversion friction.**

![Streamlined Mobile Checkout — Frictionless 1-click flow with guest checkout and upfront shipping costs](/portfolio/mor-3.png)

---

## 06 — The Transformation

**This wasn't a dramatic visual redesign. And that was the point.**

The product didn't change. The branding didn't change. The traffic didn't need to change. **The experience between intention and purchase changed.**

The new journey gave customers:

> **More clarity** &rarr; **Less hesitation** &rarr; **Fewer steps** &rarr; **Easier purchase**

#### Key Visual & UX Improvements:
- Redesigned checkout experience
- Real-time shipping-cost transparency
- 1-click guest checkout integration
- Streamlined mobile checkout journey
- Unified Cart &rarr; Checkout &rarr; Confirmation flow

The best checkout experience isn't the one customers notice. It's the one they move through without thinking about it.
