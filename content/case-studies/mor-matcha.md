## The Challenge

Every founder building a matcha brand knows the product has to do double duty — it has to look as good as it tastes, and it has to convince someone who's never tried ceremonial-grade matcha that it's worth adding to their routine. This brand had done that part right. The photography was clean, the branding felt premium, and traffic was steady. But something was breaking down at the very last step. More than seven out of every ten customers who added a product to their cart never completed the purchase.

That's not a small leak. That's hundreds of people a month who wanted the product, pulled out their card, and then quietly walked away — and for a founder who's built the brand from scratch, watching that happen in real time, order after order, is one of the hardest numbers to sit with. It doesn't feel like a UX problem. It feels personal.

When Technostripe looked closer, the checkout itself was the culprit. Shipping costs weren't shown until the very end, catching customers off guard right when they were ready to commit. The process forced an account to be created before anyone could even see a final total. And on mobile — where most of this brand's traffic lived — every extra tap was another chance to lose someone.

## The Solution

We rebuilt the checkout flow from the ground up. Shipping costs moved earlier in the journey, so there were no surprises waiting at the finish line. Guest checkout replaced the forced account creation, cutting out an unnecessary barrier entirely. And the whole flow was tightened down to the fewest steps possible, especially on mobile, where hesitation turns into abandonment the fastest.

The change wasn't dramatic on the surface — nothing about the product changed, nothing about the brand changed. But the number that had been quietly bleeding the business dry started moving in the other direction. Customers who had always wanted the product finally had a checkout that got out of their way and let them buy it.
