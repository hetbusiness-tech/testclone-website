## The Challenge

Aurra Perfume came to Technostripe with a familiar problem: they were spending steadily on Facebook and Instagram ads, the traffic looked healthy on paper, but growth had plateaued. Every month felt like starting over — new creative, new targeting, similar mediocre results. The founder could see impressions and clicks rolling in, but couldn't say with confidence what was actually driving revenue.

Where things stood before:

- CPC hovering around $1.85, high for the category
- CTR sitting at a flat 0.6% — barely above the point where an ad stops earning attention
- CAC creeping up to $48, eating deep into margins on a product with a $65 average order value
- ROAS stuck around 1.8x, barely above break-even once fulfillment and discounting were factored in
- Ad frequency climbing past 4.2, a clear sign the same small audience was seeing the same creative on repeat — textbook fatigue

## The Solution

We rebuilt the campaign structure from the ground up, moving away from broad, overlapping ad sets and into a tighter, intent-based targeting model — separating cold audiences from warm retargeting instead of blending them together and diluting both. Creative was refreshed on a rotation schedule to get ahead of fatigue rather than reacting to it, with new angles tested against scent storytelling, gifting occasions, and ingredient quality rather than one generic pitch running indefinitely.

Midway through the campaign rebuild, the numbers pointed to something the ad account alone couldn't explain: a strong share of paid traffic was reaching the site and simply not converting, even on retargeted, high-intent visitors. That led us back into the Shopify store itself, where we found the product page was slow to load on mobile and the checkout required account creation before purchase — both quietly taxing the exact traffic the ads were paying to bring in. We fixed both, tightening page speed and switching to guest checkout, so the ad spend and the site were finally working together instead of against each other.

## The Outcome

Where things landed:

- CPC down to $1.10
- CTR up to 1.9%
- CAC reduced to $21
- ROAS climbing to 5.4x
- Frequency held steady under 2.5 through consistent creative rotation, keeping the same audience engaged instead of fatigued

## The Takeaway

The lesson for the brand wasn't just "better ads." It was that ad performance and site performance are the same problem viewed from two sides — and fixing only one rarely tells the full story. A high-performing campaign feeding a slow, friction-heavy store will always plateau, no matter how good the targeting gets.
