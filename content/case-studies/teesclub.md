## The Challenge

TeesClub, a men's graphic t-shirt brand, built its whole identity around bold, specific designs — loud prints, niche pop-culture references, drops that sold out fast. The ad creative matched that energy perfectly, and people were clicking. But a huge share of that traffic showed up, looked around, and left without buying anything, even though the ad that brought them in was working exactly as intended.

Where things stood before:

- CPC around $0.95, cheap and efficient — the creative was genuinely good at getting clicks
- CTR at 2.4%, well above average for the category, proving the ads themselves weren't the problem
- CAC sitting at $38 against an average order value of $32 — losing money on nearly every first order despite strong ad performance
- ROAS at 1.3x, a confusing number given how well the ads were technically performing
- Frequency at a manageable 2.9, ruling out fatigue as the explanation

## The Solution

The disconnect became obvious once we followed the customer journey past the click: every ad, no matter which specific design or drop it featured, sent traffic to the same generic homepage. Someone who clicked an ad for a specific loud graphic tee would land on a general grid of unrelated products, forcing them to search for the exact thing that made them click in the first place. Most didn't bother — they'd already gotten what they came for, a laugh at the design, and the friction of hunting for it killed the impulse.

We rebuilt the structure so every ad linked directly to the specific product or drop-specific collection it featured, matching the promise of the click with the page behind it. Limited drops got dedicated landing pages with urgency built in — stock counters, drop countdowns — matching the fast-moving, sell-out culture the brand had built its identity around but never carried through to the site experience.

While setting this up, the data also pointed to a Shopify issue compounding the problem: the sizing chart was generic and didn't reflect the brand's relaxed, oversized fit, leading to a higher-than-normal return rate on first orders. We rebuilt the size guide to reflect the actual fit of each style, with a fit-comparison note directly on product pages, cutting down on the "ordered my normal size, it didn't fit the vibe" returns that had been quietly eating into margin.

## The Outcome

Where things landed:

- CPC held steady at $0.90, since the ads themselves never needed fixing
- CTR stayed strong at 2.3%
- CAC reduced to $14
- ROAS climbing to 5.9x
- Frequency remained healthy under 3.0, with drop-specific creative giving the account room to keep testing without repeating itself

## The Takeaway

Great ads had been doing their job the whole time. The site just wasn't finishing the sentence they started. A click is a promise — matching the landing page to exactly what the ad showed isn't a small detail, it's often the single biggest lever between traffic and revenue.
